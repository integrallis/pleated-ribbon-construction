\title{Ribbon Filter Construction at Bloom-Filter Speed:\\
\large Partition-Instead-of-Sort, and Where Layout Tricks Cannot Work}
\author{Brian Sam-Bodden}
\date{Draft v0.1 --- 2026-07-09}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Space-optimal static filters (ribbon, binary fuse) trade construction CPU for space, and that
trade gates their adoption: RocksDB measures ribbon construction at $\sim$4$\times$ the cost of
Bloom and ships Bloom by default. We present a systematic study of where memory-layout and
batching techniques can and cannot accelerate filter operations on modern out-of-order x86,
built from three pre-registered experiments with mechanical decision rules. Two are nulls with
mechanisms: point-probe batching is bound by memory-level parallelism that the out-of-order
window already saturates, and blocked-Bloom bulk construction is prefetch-hidable, capping
partitioned construction at 1.7$\times$ and refuting read-elimination applies outright. The
third localizes the one construction phase whose misses prefetch \emph{cannot} hide --- the
data-dependent row-reduction chains of homogeneous ribbon banding (5.8 misses/key at 100M
keys) --- and shows that a single-pass, cache-window \emph{partition} of the insertion stream
recovers 98\% of the locality benefit of the full start-position sort used since SGAUSS and
BuRR, at one quarter of the sorting cost, including against BuRR's own radix sorter. Sequential
construction improves 2.05--2.24$\times$ (49.6$\to$24.2\,ns/key at 100M keys). We further
observe that ribbon banding produces \emph{bit-identical} solutions under any insertion order,
which turns reordering into a provably output-neutral transformation and makes a boundary-
deferring parallel variant trivially verifiable: 4.25$\times$ banding speedup at 8 threads and
an 11.9\,ns/key total at 16 --- ribbon construction at sequential Bloom-insertion speed. Every
number derives from committed raw artifacts; protocols, amendments, and an instrument flaw
caught by cross-harness anchoring are part of the record.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Approximate-membership filters guard nearly every read path in LSM-tree storage engines, and
they are rebuilt on every flush and compaction. Ribbon filters~\cite{dillinger2021ribbon}
reduce space overhead below 10\% (BuRR below 1\%~\cite{dillinger2022burr}) versus Bloom's
44\%, but construction cost blocks adoption: RocksDB's production measurements put ribbon
construction at $\sim$140\,ns/key versus $\sim$32 for Bloom~\cite{rocksdbribbonblog}, and its
own hybrid policy exists to dodge that cost on hot levels. The construction side is an
acknowledged open problem: the parallel-BuRR authors note that ``a large portion of the
construction time is spent on parallel sorting''~\cite{becht2024parallelburr}, and recent
structures concede multi-fold construction slowdowns against optimized fuse
builders~\cite{limasset2026zor}.

This paper asks a narrow question with a broad setup: \emph{which} filter operations still
have memory-layout headroom on modern out-of-order (OoO) CPUs, and which do not? We answer it
with three pre-registered experiments (protocols, thresholds, and kill criteria frozen before
data collection; deviations recorded as amendments), and we find that the answer has a clean
mechanism: layout tricks pay exactly where misses form \emph{data-dependent chains} that
neither the OoO window nor software prefetch can overlap.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Two nulls with mechanisms} (\S\ref{sec:nulls}). Miss-dominated point probes are
bound by the per-core limit on outstanding misses; software prefetch already reaches
$\sim$88\% of that ceiling, and cross-key SIMD batching \emph{loses} to per-key scalar probing
even with all addresses L1-resident. Blocked-Bloom bulk construction misses are independent
and prefetch-hidable: radix-partitioned construction (Schmidt et
al.~\cite{schmidt2021partitioned}) caps at 1.45--1.71$\times$ against per-key insertion, a
software-pipelined prefetch loop (shipped, unevaluated, in RocksDB) recovers most of that
without transient memory, and a read-free register-aggregated apply is 1.5$\times$
\emph{slower} than partitioned RMW --- partitioning has already converted the target misses
into L2 hits.
\item \textbf{Partition-instead-of-sort for ribbon construction} (\S\ref{sec:ribbon}).
Homogeneous ribbon banding at scale pays 5.8 DRAM misses/key in dependent row-reduction
chains. A single counting pass that groups keys by L2-sized start-windows --- approximate
order only --- recovers 98.3--98.4\% of the miss reduction of the full start-position sort
(SGAUSS~\cite{dietzfelbinger2019gauss}, BuRR~\cite{dillinger2022burr}) at 5.5--6.1\,ns/key
versus 23.9--28.1 for BuRR's own ips2ra sorter, yielding end-to-end construction
2.05--2.24$\times$ faster than the reference prefetch-pipelined build. The choice of window
size barely matters (totals within 13\% across $2^{13}$--$2^{20}$ slots).
\item \textbf{Order-independence and verifiable parallelism} (\S\ref{sec:parallel}). Ribbon
banding with first-coefficient-always-one pivoting produces bit-identical solutions under any
insertion order (observed in 45/45 runs across sizes, orders, and thread counts). Reordering
is therefore provably output-neutral, and a slot-range parallel variant with boundary
deferral ($\le$0.23\% of keys) verifies itself by fingerprint: 4.25$\times$ banding speedup at
8 threads, 7.56$\times$ at 16, total 11.9\,ns/key at 100M keys.
\item \textbf{A quantified account of construction prefetch} (\S\ref{sec:prefetch}), the first
evaluation of the pipeline RocksDB ships behind a ``TODO: verify/validate'' comment: worth
1.5--1.7$\times$ on unsorted banding, and largely subsumed by partitioning.
\end{enumerate}

All measurements ran on an i9-14900HX (AVX2, no AVX-512) under pinned reference harnesses
(fastfilter\_cpp, whose ribbon implementations are the ribbon author's; BuRR; FastLanes), with
raw artifacts, a claims ledger, and automated integrity checks (information-theoretic FPR
bounds, provenance) committed alongside the code. ARM replication is prepared and pending
cloud capacity.

\section{Background}\label{sec:background}
\textbf{Ribbon banding.} A ribbon filter solves a banded GF(2) system: each key hashes to a
start column $s$ and a $w$-bit coefficient row; insertion XOR-reduces the row against existing
rows until an empty slot absorbs it~\cite{dillinger2021ribbon}. Dietzfelbinger and Walzer
showed that sorting rows by start position makes banded elimination
linear~\cite{dietzfelbinger2019gauss}; the ribbon paper's contribution was on-the-fly
insertion in \emph{arbitrary} order; BuRR reinstates a bucket sort (ips2ra) plus
bumping~\cite{dillinger2022burr}. Homogeneous ribbon never fails construction and needs no
per-key result rows. \textbf{Binary fuse} construction partially sorts keys by segment
explicitly to reduce cache misses~\cite{graf2022binaryfuse}; our Phase-0 characterization
measures that fix directly (plain xor: 9.45 misses/key; binary fuse: 1.08).

\section{Where layout tricks cannot work}\label{sec:nulls}
\subsection{Point probes are MLP-bound}
Pre-registered experiment E0 compared probe strategies over identical split-block Bloom
filters (Parquet geometry) from 32KiB to 512MiB. A vertical, lane-structured batch probe
written as plain Rust auto-vectorizes (packed-SIMD disassembly committed) yet loses to per-key
scalar probing at \emph{every} size --- even with all addresses forced L1-hot (555 vs.\ 665
Mops/s), because the split-block check is already horizontally SIMD within one key and AVX2
gathers cannot beat eight independent scalar loads that the OoO window overlaps for free. At
RAM sizes the same instruction stream runs 5--6$\times$ faster when L1-hot: probing is bound
by memory-level parallelism, and software prefetch reaches $\sim$88\% of the single-core
outstanding-miss ceiling. There is no compute bottleneck for a layout to remove. % C2,C6,C7

\subsection{Blocked-Bloom construction is prefetch-hidable}
Experiment E1a measured five bulk builders at 1M--429M keys with a bit-identity gate (OR
commutativity). Per-key insertion degrades to one random read-modify-write miss per key at
scale (11.5\,ns/key at 100M; IPC 0.48)~% C9
; radix-partitioned construction in the style of Schmidt et al.~\cite{schmidt2021partitioned}
reaches only 1.45--1.71$\times$ --- and a software-pipelined prefetch insert (RocksDB's shape)
achieves 6.1--8.9\,ns/key with \emph{zero} transient memory, beating partitioning up to 100M
keys. % C10,C13
A read-free apply --- partition pre-merged (block, mask) pairs, OR-aggregate each block in
registers, exactly one store per block, never read filter memory --- is 1.5$\times$
\emph{slower} than partitioned RMW, with non-temporal stores changing nothing: partitioning
already converted the target misses into L2 hits, so the second grouping pass buys back only
L2 latency. % C11
Bloom construction misses are \emph{independent}; the memory system hides them.

\section{Partition-instead-of-sort for ribbon}\label{sec:ribbon}
\subsection{The target, measured}
Phase-0 characterization (reference fastfilter\_cpp binary, 3 reps) shows homogeneous ribbon
construction at 100M keys costs 50.6\,ns/key at IPC 0.64 with \textbf{5.81 cache-misses/key}
--- banding walks a random start per key through a 1.3GB working set, and each row-reduction
step depends on the previous XOR, so prefetch cannot dissolve the chain the way it does
Bloom's independent probes. % C14
\subsection{Design}
We permute only the \emph{input}: a single counting pass buckets keys by
$\lfloor s/2^{16}\rfloor$ (windows of $2^{16}$ slots $\approx$ 768KiB of banding state, under
half an L2), recomputing the hash rather than materializing pairs; a second pass emits keys in
window order. The unmodified reference kernel (its prefetch pipeline included) then consumes
the permuted array. Everything downstream --- banding, back-substitution, queries --- is the
ribbon author's code.
\subsection{Results}
Table~\ref{tab:ribbon} (100M keys; 400M in artifacts). The partition pass costs
5.5--6.1\,ns/key against 23.9--28.1 for ips2ra in our driver (17.3 inside BuRR's own bench);
banding lands within 13\% of banding-after-full-sort (15.2 vs.\ 13.4\,ns/key), i.e., the
partition recovers 98.3--98.4\% of the sort's miss reduction (5.75$\to$0.17 vs.\ $\to$0.075
misses/key). End-to-end: 24.2 vs.\ 49.6 (reference) and 40.7 (best full sort). As an anchor,
BuRR end-to-end builds at $\sim$70\,ns/key on this machine (different structure: $r{=}8$,
$\sim$1\% space overhead, bumping); our homogeneous configuration is 7.63 bits/key at 0.81\%
FPR ($\sim$9.5\% overhead). % C16,C17,C20,C21
A window sweep across $2^{13}$--$2^{20}$ slots moves totals only between 23.8 and
26.9\,ns/key: the technique has no tuning cliff, and our registered result (at $2^{16}$) was
conservative --- $2^{14}$ is mildly better. % C22

\section{Order-independence and parallel construction}\label{sec:parallel}
Across all 45 sequential runs and every parallel run (sizes to 400M, five orderings, thread
counts to 16), the banded solution is \emph{byte-identical} under an FNV fingerprint:
first-coefficient-always-one banding computes a canonical form of the system regardless of
insertion order. Reordering is thus provably output-neutral --- the linear-algebra analogue of
OR-commutativity --- and it converts parallel-construction verification from a distributed-
systems problem into a checksum. Our parallel variant assigns disjoint window ranges (split at
equal key counts) to threads; keys starting within $2^{14}$ slots of a range boundary defer to
a sequential tail (0.015--0.226\% of keys), following parallel BuRR's boundary
idea~\cite{becht2024parallelburr}. Banding scales 1.81/2.83/4.25/7.56$\times$ at 2/4/8/16
threads; the residual is Amdahl's: the sequential counting pass (5.7) and back-substitution
(3.4\,ns/key) now dominate the 11.9\,ns/key total. Both are parallelizable (counting sort
trivially; back-substitution as in parallel BuRR) --- future work. % C23

\section{Construction prefetch, evaluated}\label{sec:prefetch}
RocksDB ships a software-pipelined construction prefetch behind a comment reading ``TODO:
verify/validate''; the reference harness enables the same pipeline above 1500 slots. Our
no-prefetch control isolates it: 1.5--1.7$\times$ on unsorted banding (76.9$\to$46.1\,ns/key
at 100M). It attacks the \emph{start-locating} miss but cannot touch the dependent reduction
chain, which is why partitioning strictly dominates it here while the analogous prefetch
strategy \emph{wins} for Bloom construction (\S\ref{sec:nulls}). % C18,C13

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
Sorted banded elimination: \cite{dietzfelbinger2019gauss,dillinger2022burr,becht2024parallelburr}.
Arbitrary-order banding and production engineering: \cite{dillinger2021ribbon,rocksdbribbonblog}.
Cache-conscious filter construction: buffered Bloom filters~\cite{canim2010buffered},
radix-partitioned filters~\cite{schmidt2021partitioned}, binary-fuse segment
sort~\cite{graf2022binaryfuse}, xor-filter buffered scatter~\cite{graf2020xor}; the generic
two-phase partition-then-accumulate pattern appears as propagation
blocking~\cite{beamer2017propagation} with write-combining from~\cite{wassenberg2011radix}.
SIMD probe-side layouts: \cite{polychroniou2014vectorized,lang2019performance,apache2021sbbf}.
Our delta to BuRR is precise: BuRR sorts fully (ips2ra) to enable threshold-based bumping and
tighter space; we show that for homogeneous ribbon --- the variant RocksDB ships --- a
counting-pass partition captures 98\% of the sort's locality at 4$\times$ less reorder cost,
with output-neutrality making the transformation and its parallelization verifiable.

\section{Limitations and threats to validity}\label{sec:limits}
Single x86 machine so far (AVX2 Raptor Lake); ARM replication is scripted and pending cloud
capacity, and all claims are scoped accordingly. We accelerate \emph{homogeneous} ribbon
($\sim$9.5\% space overhead at $r{=}7$), not BuRR's sub-1\%-overhead structure; the BuRR
comparison is an anchor, not apples-to-apples. Sort baselines include BuRR's own sequential
ips2ra but not its parallel form; our parallel comparison is scoped to banding. Bit-identity
of banding under reordering is an empirical observation with an informal argument (canonical
banded form under a fixed pivot rule); we do not prove it. LSM end-to-end integration
(build-during-compaction) is future work.

\section{Conclusion}
On modern OoO x86, memory-layout optimization for filters pays in exactly one place we could
find --- and it is the place that matters: the dependent-miss chains of ribbon construction,
the very cost that keeps space-optimal filters out of production write paths. A one-pass
partition closes 98\% of the gap the literature closes with a full sort, at a quarter of the
price; order-independence makes it safe and its parallelization checkable; and 16 threads
build ribbon filters as fast as one thread inserts into a Bloom filter. The two nulls are not
failures of the method but the map that located the win.
